## Bootstrap reality

Gitea 28.3.0 runs on host loopback (F-06.1). The planner, builder, reviewer and
verifier bot accounts and their tokens exist since S-01.2.2, in the Owner account's
Credential Manager (`AIEH Gitea bot <role>`) pending the dispatcher, but no agent
workload has run or held one (decision 0003, C6), and the Owner's account performs
every operation that has been performed: the Owner directs the work and approves privileged host acts by UAC consent,
host tooling under the Owner's account runs the guest lifecycle and the Agent Server
management operations, and the host-side bootstrap agents share that account. They are
therefore not a role: they are granted nothing, they inherit the reach of the Owner's
account (`fd-bootstrap-shared-identity`), and the sunset map below names the leaf at
which each operation they perform today moves to its planned principal.

## How to read it

- **Roles are grants; principals are runtime identities.** Each role binds to exactly
  one principal of the inventory, and a principal may carry several roles (the planner
  and the builder are both roles on `engineering-agent`, each with its own Gitea bot
  identity and token). A grant names an inventory operation (`op-*`), a capability
  (`cap-*`, defined here) or an inventory credential (`cred-*`).
- **A role is agent-controlled** when its principal is (`planner`, `builder`,
  `reviewer`, `ci-job`). The checker derives this from the inventory.
- **Denials** are listed explicitly. Every agent-controlled role denies at least the
  baseline: `op-protected-merge`, `cap-code-merge-integration`,
  `cap-protected-ref-push`, `op-gitea-administration`, `op-effect-apply`,
  `op-effect-receipt-write`, `cred-executor-identity`,
  `cred-agent-server-session-key`, `op-as-conversation-create`,
  `cred-moby-docker-group`, `cred-gitea-owner-account` and `cred-gitea-admin`.
- **Statuses** follow the inventory's rules (`planned` with sources, `observed` with
  evidence, `unknown` with a finding). A role is `observed` only where its grants are
  shown by committed evidence today.
- **Realization** says how a role is to be enforced. A row is `verified: true` only
  with evidence; every other row says `verified: false`, names the leaf that is to
  verify it, and its note says why it is not verified (unknown, not applicable, or
  blocked).

## Issuance and revocation contract

- **Issuance.** `gitea-site-admin` issues Gitea identities, bot accounts and their
  tokens, protection rules and runner registration secrets. `management-dispatcher`,
  the launcher and credential broker, is custodian of the standing bot credentials. For
  a confirmed attempt it borrows the stage's mapped credential into its own attempt
  context only, never the conversation container (decision 0013, point 7), and releases
  that in-memory access when the operation ends; the release is not Gitea revocation
  (decision 0007 points 4 and 5, amendment 1). Gitea 28.3.0 cannot bound a token to an
  attempt (it has no token lifetime, S-01.2.2) and gives the dispatcher no replacement,
  so issuance, rotation and revocation of a standing bot credential stay with the site
  administrator's lifecycle (`gitea_roles.py` provision, rotate, retire), on the triggers
  below and never on routine attempt completion. The dispatcher holds no administrator
  credential and issues nothing. Tokens never go into an image, seed, repository, log or
  record: records carry fingerprints only. Owner-class and
  executor-class credentials are issued only by Owner acts. The executor issues
  nothing. The inference broker alone issues inference access, and no provider
  credential is ever a role token.
- **Triggers:** attempt end, suspected exposure, definition revocation, policy revision,
  expiry and Owner direction. Every credential row includes suspected exposure and
  Owner direction. Attempt end is a trigger only of a credential issued for a single
  attempt (the inference attempt token, the conversation keys, the CI job tokens); an
  agent role's such credentials end with the attempt. A standing credential the Gitea
  site administrator issues never names attempt end (decision 0007, amendment 1).
- **Revoker:** the issuer's role. Where the issuer is outside the inventory (a provider,
  Gitea Actions, the Gitea installation), the row names the role that revokes.
- **On new dispatch:** immediate refusal.
- **In flight:** the dispatcher stops or contains the attempts holding the credential
  through `op-as-runtime-stop` and records them as exposed; Gitea fails closed at the
  credential's next authenticated call; CI jobs holding it are cancelled.
- **Evidence:** a Gitea audit record where audit logging is enabled (it is off by
  default), plus a revocation receipt in the integration store with the fingerprint,
  role, trigger, revoker and affected attempts. A receipt goes to the effects store only
  when the revocation was itself an executor effect (decision 0004, point 1): the
  executor's own credentials in the target.
- **Privileged rotation.** Rotating an Owner, admin, executor or management credential
  is a consequential external effect: an Owner-only manifest applied by the executor in
  the target, or an Owner act recorded in the bootstrap log during bootstrap.
- **Rotation owner.** Each row names one, and it equals the credential's
  `rotation_revocation_owner` in the inventory.

## Network matrix (S-01.4)

Plan leaf **S-01.4**, "Apply deny-by-default inter-domain communications", as of
2026-11-07 (baseline commit `ea10669`). It describes the minimal allowlist between the
Owner UI, the Integration Service, the Agent Server, the containers, CI and the privileged
executor, and the inspection that shows each path.

- **Acceptance of this leaf:** unexpected management and credential access is denied while
  required workflows still communicate.
- **Machine-readable form:** [`network-matrix.json`](network-matrix.json). The tables below
  are rendered from it, and
  [`tools/check_network_matrix.py`](../tools/check_network_matrix.py) checks it (`task
  policy:network-check`).
- **Decision:** [0013](../decisions/0013-owner-ui-session-access-and-management-key-custody.md)
  (Proposed): the Owner UI never holds the management key and reaches a session only with
  that conversation's runtime credential; same-account host processes are outside the
  threat model on the host network plane too, so host-internal edges are disclosed, not
  prevented; untrusted code has no network path to Gitea.
- **This matrix opens nothing.** No forward, firewall rule or Gitea change is made. The one
  control S-01.4 adds is the guest-level container egress guard
  (`provisioning.agent_server.admission.network.egress_guard` of the QEMU profile): every new
  forwarded connection from a container bridge is logged and refused, with an empty
  allowlist. The inference class is never an entry: its endpoint is a guest listener on
  the docker0 gateway address, reached through INPUT and allowed by the container
  guard's one ACCEPT (`network.inference_composition`), and the allowlist stays empty
  with it (the profile's `allowlist_rule`; decision 0015 section 4.2, which refines
  S-01.4 ruling R5).

### How to read it

- **Rows are keyed by ordered pairs** of the inventory's trust domains (`td-*`), a domain
  with itself included, and every pair has at least one row. `external_edges` hold the
  paths between a domain and what lies outside every domain (`external:*`).
- **Verdicts:** `allow` (a required path with at least one prevention mechanism), `deny`
  (refused, with the enforcement that refuses it) and `disclosed` (reachable under an
  accepted scope, with a finding).
- **Statuses:** `observed` (committed evidence and an inspection command), `planned` (the
  plan leaf that will observe it, `verify_at`, and where known the leaf that builds it) and
  `decided` (a decision record and the leaf that builds it).
- **Endpoints** are inventory assets or profiles P7 endpoint classes. Every inventory asset
  has a class in `asset_classes`; an API, socket, privileged unit, secret file or credential
  store, or an asset that holds a privileged credential, is a management or credential
  asset.
- **Owner act** marks a path that needs a recorded Owner approval (a new host port, a
  guestfwd, a host listener): an observed one cites the approval in the bootstrap log.

### What the checker enforces

`python -I -B tools/check_network_matrix.py` (`task policy:network-check`) exits 0 only
when:

1. every ordered pair of trust domains has a row, every untrusted or contained domain has
   an external row, and every row is classified (a known verdict, status, protocol,
   endpoint and enforcement kind and place; no unknown key);
2. an `allow` from an untrusted or contained domain into a trusted one (or the trusted
   computing base) names a profiles P7 endpoint class other than `management`, never a
   management or credential asset, and no credential of privileged authority;
3. every `allow` names a prevention mechanism, every `disclosed` row a finding and every
   `deny` its enforcement;
4. every id resolves: domains, externals, assets (each with a class consistent with its kind
   and credentials), credentials, findings, plan leaves and profile endpoints;
5. an `observed` row has evidence and an inspection command, a `planned` row the plan leaf
   that will observe it (`verify_at`) and a `decided` row a decision source and the leaf
   that builds it;
6. every endpoint and `launch.argv` forward of an active profile (one whose provider
   validation passed) appears in an `allow` row;
7. an `observed` Owner act cites its bootstrap-log approval, verbatim;
8. no machine path appears, this README names every edge id and its generated tables equal
   `--render`.

`--list` prints each edge with its verdict and status; `--render` prints the generated
tables. The negative cases are in
[`tools/tests/test_check_network_matrix.py`](../tools/tests/test_check_network_matrix.py).
When the matrix changes, edit the JSON, replace the generated block with the output of
`--render`, and run the checker.

Plan leaf **Q-02.1**, "Define hostile-CI threat model and permission ceiling", as of
2026-11-10 (baseline commit `93d49f6`). It inventories the Gitea runner's trust, the
repository, fork and PR tokens, the secrets, the network and the privileged host surfaces, and
specifies separate validation and trusted promotion lanes.

- **Acceptance of this leaf:** no untrusted PR validation job has any necessary reason or
  configuration path to production or Owner credentials; the CI runner's reachability to Gitea
  and the job token's use are decided against decision 0013 point 7; every forward is an Owner
  act.
- **Machine-readable form:** the CI runner control profile
  [`ci-runner-profile.json`](ci-runner-profile.json), the token and secret policy
  [`ci-token-secret-policy.json`](ci-token-secret-policy.json) and the governance workflow's
  source of record [`ci/workflows/validation.yml`](ci/workflows/validation.yml). The tables
  below are rendered from the two JSON files, and
  [`tools/check_ci_policy.py`](../tools/check_ci_policy.py) checks all three (`task
  policy:ci-check`).
- **Decision:** 0016, hostile-CI threat model, the validation and promotion lanes, and the CI
  runner's reach to Gitea (Proposed; the Overseer commits the record under
  [`decisions/`](../decisions/)). Both JSON files name it by id; the checker fails once its
  record exists and is not linked.
- **This policy opens nothing.** No runner, CI guest, forward, listener, Gitea setting, secret,
  repository or workload exists because of it. Every act it needs is listed under
  [Owner acts](#owner-acts) with the leaf that requests it.
- **Evidence:** [`evidence/Q-02.1-hostile-ci-policy.md`](../evidence/Q-02.1-hostile-ci-policy.md):
  the Gitea Actions facts read in the pinned source archive (not live), and the build.

### The design in brief

- **Two lanes.** The validation lane is the CI guest (`td-ci-runner-host`, contained) and the
  disposable job containers in it (`td-untrusted-ci`, untrusted). It holds the runner token on
  the guest and the two tokens Gitea mints per task in the job, nothing else. The promotion lane
  is trusted: the reporter, the Owner's protected merge, the verifier and the executor. They
  share only the exact revision: the SHA and the digest of the bundle that carried it.
- **The runner reaches Gitea; the job reaches nothing.** The runner's only path off the CI guest
  is the `ci-control` forward, a dispatcher-held SSH remote forward bound to the guest's
  loopback, an Owner act. A job has no network path at all (decision 0013, point 7, unchanged):
  the proposal arrives as a verified bundle staged into a read-only mount.
- **The job token is permissionless and inert.** Gitea 28.3.0 has job-token ceilings: restricted
  mode and an all-none ceiling at the governance owner and repository, with `permissions: {}`
  in the workflow, leave it no permission on any private repository. Every public repository
  stays readable whatever the ceiling, so none may exist.
- **No secret or variable exists anywhere**, because Gitea delivers every owner and repository
  secret to every task, and every variable, global ones included.
- **The workflow is the governance repository's**, dispatched from its protected default branch
  by `aieh-bot-ci`; Actions is disabled on every repository that receives proposals, so a
  proposal's own workflows, scoped ones included, never run. The required context
  `aieh/validation` is posted by the reporter only after it reads the run record.

## Test budgets (F-09.1)

Since the Owner's direction of 2026-11-11 (test runtime is diagnostic, not a gate),
[`test-budgets.json`](test-budgets.json) holds no budgets: it records the measurements of the
reference profile in [`evidence/test-profile/`](../evidence/test-profile/) (tests, launches,
`mkdtemp` calls and median wall times, per category, per suite and in total) and the Owner's
investigation signals (a median roughly 50% above its measurement, a full suite above five
minutes). `task test:budget` (`python -I -B tools/check_test_budget.py check --run`) reports
them as notes and never fails on them; it fails on failing, removed or newly skipped tests
(against the recorded test set,
[`evidence/test-profile/test-ids.json`](../evidence/test-profile/test-ids.json)), on a class
without a valid category and on a `unit` class that is not isolated. It is an acceptance
obligation at every leaf acceptance and at release, never a hook. New tests need no change to
either file; the recorded set is re-recorded only when tests are deliberately removed, renamed or
skipped. The full description is in
[docs/development.md, Testing](../docs/development.md#testing).
