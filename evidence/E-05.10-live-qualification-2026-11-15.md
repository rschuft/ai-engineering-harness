## The host-side observer, and rule revision 4

Around each tunnel row the runner recorded every TCP row of the host to remote port 443 (any owner, TIME_WAIT
included) before and after the row (`host_443`), judged after the run (`11-observer-analysis.json` for the
job-access-probe pass, `13-observer-analysis.json` for the catalogue's).

- In every pass each admitted row shows exactly one new connection, owned by the tunnel's process, to
  `[2607:6bc0::10]:443`; the IP-literal, other-port, plain-HTTP and both server-name rows show none.
- **Revision 4 (the engineering manager's ruling, a post-hoc correction; both readings kept):** a pid-0 TIME_WAIT row
  whose (local, remote) endpoints the tunnel's process owned in the `before` snapshot is that connection closing, not
  a new connection. Under revision 3 as written (rows compared with their state), the "another host" row in each
  job read **UNKNOWN**: its one "new" row was the preceding admitted connection's own close (the same local port,
  owned by the tunnel's process in FIN_WAIT1 in that row's `before`). Under revision 4 those rows read **PASS**.
  The denial evidence independent of the tunnel's log is primarily the job's own observations; the observer
  corroborates (it cannot see a short connection the origin closed first).
