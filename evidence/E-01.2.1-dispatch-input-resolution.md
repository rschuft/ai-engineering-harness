  Work, repository and design, and the agent catalog pin, to a declared execution profile;

## 2. The contract

- **Request** (`ai-engineering-harness/dispatch/request/v1`): `format`, `work_id`,
  `repository`, `stage`, `profile`, `catalog_pin`; all required, no other field. Grammars:
  `work_id` the planning adapter's `TASK_ID_RE` (at most 64, canonical upper case); the others
  `[a-z0-9]+(-[a-z0-9]+)*` (at most 64), the stage then in the store's `STAGES`. A field whose
  name tries to supply a path, mount, digest, resource, runtime, revision or ordinal is
  `REQUEST_OVERRIDE_FORBIDDEN`; any other extra field `REQUEST_UNKNOWN_FIELD`; a path or other
  value outside a grammar `REQUEST_FIELD_INVALID`. The resolver has no parameter through which
  a caller could pass a path, digest or runtime choice.
- **Order of checks**, first failure refuses: request; repository approved; stage known;
  stage bound for the repository; profile approved and bound; at least one approved pin, this
  pin approved and bound; profile file (sha256 equal to the approved one, profile/v1 schema,
  its own name, `status.operational` exactly `true`); the Work (listed by Backlog.md, and
  Backlog.md's own readiness through `read_readiness`, which also resolves every pointer in
  git); the design (every pointer resolved, one change at one commit, equal to the accepted
  revision in the manifest); the ordinal. Checks 1 to 6 read only the manifest.
- **Record** (`ai-engineering-harness/dispatch/resolved-inputs/v1`, frozen): `work_id`,
  `repository`, `stage`, `ordinal`, `design_revision`, `profile`, `profile_digest`,
  `catalog_pin`, `agent_manifest_digest`, `planned_workspace`, `policy_digest`,
  `policy_inputs`, and `approval` (manifest schema, `version`, sha256 of its bytes).
  `attempt_identity()` gives the store's `AttemptIdentity`; `intent()` gives
  `{"dispatchInputs": <record>}`, so the stored intent and its digest carry the provenance.
- **`planned_workspace`**, the fixed rule: `<repository>/<work_id>/<attempt_id>`, relative to
  the `workspaces` category, with `attempt_id = uuid5(ATTEMPT_NAMESPACE, operation_key)` from
  the store. Each attempt (ordinal) has its own; a rework never reuses a workspace.
- **`policy_digest`** = sha256 of the canonical JSON (sorted keys, no whitespace, UTF-8,
  `storecore.canonical_json`) of `policy_inputs`:
  `{"format": "ai-engineering-harness/dispatch/policy-inputs/v1", "repository": {"id"},
  "stage", "design": {"repository", "change", "commit"}, "profile": {"id", "sha256"},
  "catalog_pin": {"id", "repository", "commit", "manifest_sha256"}}`: exactly the approved
  entries the attempt was admitted under. Not covered: the manifest version and digest
  (provenance, beside it), the binding lists, the Work and the ordinal (the identity). A
  manifest revision that leaves these entries alone keeps the policy digest but changes the
  intent digest, so retrying a reserved key under another approval is
  `ConflictingReservation` (tested).
- **`agent_manifest_digest` becomes required at dispatch admission**, as the leaf was asked
  to decide: the resolver never produces a null one. The store column stays nullable (no
  migration; the store's own comment "E-01.2.1 makes it required" now means the dispatch
  path). Consequence, intended: until A-01.1 promotes an approved catalog pin into the live
  manifest, every dispatch is refused (`NO_APPROVED_CATALOG`, `CATALOG_PIN_REQUIRED`).
- **The ordinal** is not the caller's: `next_ordinal(work_id, design_revision, stage)` is an
  injected trusted reader (`store_next_ordinal` reads `claims`, highest plus one).
  `reserve_attempt` stays the arbiter: a live claim or a race is refused there (tested).

## 3. The approved-input manifest

Schema `ai-engineering-harness/dispatch/approved-inputs/v1`: `schema`, `version` (integer
from 1), `repositories` [`id`], `designs` [`repository`, `change`, `commit`], `profiles`
[`id`, `sha256`], `catalog_pins` [`id`, `repository`, `commit`, `manifest_sha256`],
`bindings` [`repository`, `stage`, `profiles`, `catalog_pins`]. Every key required, none
other, at every level; strict JSON (duplicate keys, NaN and Infinity refused); at most 1 MiB.
Checker codes: `MANIFEST_SCHEMA`, `MANIFEST_VERSION`, `MANIFEST_ID_INVALID`,
`MANIFEST_COMMIT_NOT_FULL` (40 or 64 lower-case hex), `MANIFEST_DIGEST_INVALID` (64
lower-case hex, no `sha256:` prefix), `MANIFEST_STAGE_UNKNOWN`, `MANIFEST_DUPLICATE`,
`MANIFEST_UNKNOWN_REFERENCE`, `MANIFEST_JSON`.

**Custody.** Decision 0009 point 1 places "authorization records" under `persistent/`;
point 2 makes components consume the resolver's categories and invent none. The manifest is
therefore `<state>/dispatch/approved-inputs.json` (the `state` category, layout
`<component>/`), computed by `manifest_path()` through `aieh_paths`; nothing is created.
`load_manifest` refuses a relative path, `.` or `..` parts, and a path inside or containing
the checkout, before reading and again after resolving links. The request never names a
manifest. Point 11 and decision 0010: placement is not the boundary; the guest, the mount
policy, bundle exchange and the role-token matrix keep agents from the managed root, and the
bootstrap limit `fd-bootstrap-shared-identity` stands (host processes of the Owner's account
can change the file; the sha256 in each reservation makes a change visible afterwards). The
repository holds only the schema check, the synthetic example and fixtures. No live manifest
was written.

## 7. Decisions taken inside the dispatch, and findings for the Overseer

- **F1, accepted design revision (a choice the authority does not settle; for ruling).** The
  blueprint persists acceptance into OpenSpec ("Record acceptance ... WRITE · Accepted design
  revision") and says a card's status is "not an authoritative new 'design approved' bit". No
  mechanism yet marks an OpenSpec change accepted (the `intent-driven` schema records
  acceptance per constraint, not per change), and the dispatch's manifest list had no entry
  that could answer "the accepted one". Within design point 2 (the manifest is the approval
  source), the manifest's `designs` entries name one accepted commit per (repository,
  change), and a task whose pointers pin another commit is `DESIGN_REVISION_MISMATCH`. The
  alternative the plan's adoption column hints at ("OpenSpec/Git refs") would be reachability
  from a protected ref of the repository; it needs a trusted clone per repository and a
  definition of "latest accepted", and was not built. If the Overseer prefers it, only
  `resolve`'s design step and the `designs` section change.
- **F2, qualification of the profile.** Dispatch step 01 asks for "a qualified profile";
  contract C6 says a profile is not operational until its gates pass. The resolver requires
  `status.operational` to be exactly `true`, in addition to the approved digest. Both
  checked-in profiles declare `false` today, so they are refused even when approved (tested).
- **F3, the manifest's category.** The `state` category is class B ("installed harness and
  its managed data"); an authorization record is closer to class E ("protected configuration,
  credentials and evidence"), whose only category today is `gitea`. A dedicated class-E
  category would be a change to `integrations/paths/` (out of bounds here); placement is not
  the security boundary either way (decision 0010).
- **F4, the attempt key has no repository.** The store's key is
  `attempt/v1/<work_id>/<change>@<commit>/<stage>/<ordinal>`. Two approved repositories whose
  Backlog.md ids, change ids and design commits all coincide would share a key. A design
  commit is repository content, so this needs the same commit in both (a fork or mirror). The
  planned workspace includes the repository. Not repaired (the store is out of bounds).
- **F5, documents outside the bounds.** `AGENTS.md` lists the setup commands; it was not
  edited (out of bounds) and now omits `dispatch:test` (it already omitted `gitea:test`).
  The README's status sections were not edited.

## 8. What is carried

- **A-01.1 (protected catalog):** produces the approved catalog pins (repository, full commit,
  agent/Skill manifest sha256) and writes them into the live manifest through its protected
  promotion. Until then dispatch is refused, by design. Verifying that the catalog's manifest
  at the pinned commit has that sha256, and materializing it read-only, belongs to A-01.x and
  E-01.3.x; the resolver records the approved digest only.
- **Governance:** who approves and writes the live manifest's repository, design, profile and
  binding entries, through which act and with which record (decision 0007's issuance pattern
  suggests an Owner act during bootstrap). Also the design-acceptance mechanism of F1 and,
  if wanted, a class-E category (F3).
- **E-01.2.2 (ceilings):** resolves `planned_workspace` to a host path under the `workspaces`
  category and refuses one outside it or one the grammar cannot see (Windows reserved names
  such as `con`, which the identifier grammar allows); validates the mount list, CPU and memory
  caps and the required Docker runtime against the profile and the manifest; extends
  `policy_inputs` (a new `POLICY_FORMAT`) and the refusal set in this module. The S-02 items
  carried to E-01.2.2 (the drills and the stored-state question) are untouched here.
- **E-01.3.x:** the dispatcher that calls `resolve` then `reserve_attempt`, constructs the live
  `PlanningWorkSource` (Backlog.md and a trusted clone per approved repository), maps the
  stage to its role credential (decision 0007), and reconciles a reservation after a crash
  instead of re-resolving it (a re-resolution asks for the next ordinal and meets the live
  claim).
