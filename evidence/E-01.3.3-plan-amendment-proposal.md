## 1. Why the text changes

- **The dispatcher principal (ruling F-2).** "The dispatcher principal" has three senses that the proof conflates:
  the code path (the dispatch module as the only path for op-as-* on Work), the guest login that reads the key
  (today `agent`), and a host identity distinct from the Owner's account, which no leaf builds. Decision 0013 point 1
  makes the bootstrap dispatcher the Owner's account. With one host account, a second guest login would be a second
  door on the same key ring, so "a read by the operator login (after the cut-over) is refused" cannot be shown
  without custody theatre. The ruling: the bootstrap reading as written; the key file stays `root:agent 0440` (R9);
  the cut-over is the code path; the operator-login separation waits for the dispatcher's own host identity.
- **The standing bot credential (ruling F-1, as corrected by the Owner).** Gitea 28.3.0 cannot bound a token to an
  attempt and gives the dispatcher no way to issue a replacement (the CLI is the site administrator's). The Owner
  separates three lifecycles: (1) the standing bot credential, issued, rotated and revoked only by the existing
  administrative authority (`gitea_roles.py provision`, `rotate`, `retire`) on defined security and lifecycle
  triggers, never on routine attempt completion; (2) its per-attempt use, borrowed into the dispatcher's bounded
  attempt context for a confirmed attempt and released when the operation ends, which is not Gitea revocation; (3)
  the per-attempt inference token (decision 0015), issued by the broker and revoked at attempt end, unchanged.
  "Rotate role tokens per attempt where Gitea allows it" does not describe that, and neither do the leaf's title
  ("per-attempt role tokens") and artifact ("per-attempt delivery and rotation").
- **Decision 0007 (the Owner's reconciliation).** Its point 4 says the dispatcher delivers role tokens into the
  attempt environment and that they are otherwise rotated at attempt end; its point 5 lists attempt end among the
  revocation triggers without distinguishing credential classes. The Owner asks for all three statements to be
  reconciled by an explicit, authorized amendment that keeps the historical text, and for no contradictory current
  requirement to remain: section 5 is the amendment, section 6 the records that restate the superseded clauses.
- **The UI clause (ruling F-3).** "A read by the UI is refused" is read as the UI's credential class: a conversation
  runtime credential minted by the dispatcher is refused on every key-gated management route and admitted only by
  its conversation's inner server. The host copy of the key is the disclosed limit (decision 0013 point 6); the UI's
  forward channel stays U-03.1's.

## 5. Decision 0007 amendment (the Overseer's draft, exact)

To be appended to `decisions/0007-role-token-matrix-and-revocation-contract.md`, after its last section, on the
Owner's approval; `<DATE>` becomes the date of that approval. The text between the fences is the Overseer's draft

````markdown
## Amendment 1 (OA-20): credential classes, borrowing and revocation triggers

Added with the Owner's approval of <DATE> (Owner decision OA-20), following the Owner's directions of 2026-11-11
on the E-01.3.3 token lifecycle and on this decision (both verbatim in `evidence/bootstrap-log.md`). Points 4 and
5 above are kept as decided on 2026-11-07, for history and provenance. Where they differ from this amendment, this
amendment governs. The superseded clauses are named exactly below; no other part of the decision changes.

1. **Point 4, superseded:** "delivers each agent role token per attempt into the attempt environment only".
   Decision 0013 point 7 had already relocated the attempt environment to the dispatcher's attempt context.
   **Current requirement:**
   - The standing Gitea bot credentials stay on the trusted host, in the OS credential provider.
   - For a confirmed operation, the dispatcher borrows the stage's mapped credential into its own in-memory
     attempt context. It borrows only that stage's credential and releases the access when the operation ends.
   - A standing Gitea token never enters a guest, an agent environment, a container, a conversation, a
     conversation record or the UI. Records carry fingerprints only.
2. **Point 4, superseded:** "the token is bounded to the attempt where the pinned Gitea supports it (to verify)
   and is otherwise rotated at attempt end".
   - **What was established.** S-01.2.2 verified that Gitea 28.3.0 gives a token no lifetime and that the bots
     have no password. A replacement token can therefore be issued only by the site administrator, never by the
     dispatcher. The Owner's direction of 2026-11-11 rejects routine revocation without an authorized, automatic
     replacement mechanism.
   - **Current requirement:**
     - Releasing the dispatcher's in-memory access at the end of an operation is not Gitea revocation. It is
       recorded as a release.
     - Issuance (point 4), rotation and revocation (point 5, "the issuer's role") of a standing Gitea bot
       credential are the Gitea site administrator's. During bootstrap the administration command performs them
       as the Owner's account.
     - Rotation and revocation happen on the triggers of point 5 as amended in 3 below, never on routine attempt
       completion.
     - The dispatcher holds no administrator credential and issues nothing.
3. **Point 5, narrowed by credential class:** "attempt end" in the list of trigger classes.
   - **Credentials with attempt end as a trigger:** attempt end remains a trigger for credentials issued for a
     single attempt, task or session. Among them:
     - the broker-issued inference attempt token (decision 0015);
     - the inner conversation keys, which end with their container;
     - the CI job and runtime tokens Gitea Actions mints per task;
     - the conversation runtime credential.
   - **Standing Gitea bot credentials:** attempt end is not a trigger for them. They are revoked or rotated only on
     suspected exposure, definition revocation, policy revision, expiry or Owner direction.
   - **Effects of a security-triggered revocation.** Revoking a standing credential keeps all of point 5's common
     effects:
     - new dispatch is refused at once;
     - Gitea fails closed at the next authenticated call, so delivery is refused until the administrator-managed
       lifecycle restores the credential;
     - attempts in flight that hold the credential are stopped or contained and recorded as exposed;
     - CI jobs holding it are cancelled;
     - the evidence is the Gitea audit record where enabled, plus the revocation receipt in the integration store.

Disclosed, not prevented: during bootstrap the Owner's account can read the standing credentials in the
credential provider (decision 0013 point 6).
````

**The rule, by credential class.** A credential the Gitea site administrator issues (inventory `issuer`
`gitea-site-admin`) is standing; any other credential of an agent role is issued for a single attempt (the inference
attempt token, the conversation keys, the CI job and runtime tokens). Attempt end is **required** on an agent role's
single-attempt credential (as before) and **forbidden** on a standing one, on any role's row. Forbidden rather than
merely not required: the amendment says standing credentials are rotated or revoked "only on" the other triggers and
"never on routine attempt completion", and a forbidden trigger makes the checker refuse a row that would bring the
superseded rotation back; "not required" would leave that to review. The class comes from a field the inventory
already has, so the schema does not change. Today the class holds the eight credentials the site administrator issues (the four bot
tokens, the verifier's and the protection auditor's tokens, the runner registration token and the Owner's Gitea
account); none names attempt end after the patch.

**`rotation_owner`.** In the matrix, the rotation owner is the role that rotates the credential (decision 0007
point 5; the checker ties its principal to the inventory's `rotation_revocation_owner`). Under the amendment the
dispatcher neither rotates nor triggers rotation of a standing bot credential: it borrows and releases it, which the
matrix already records as `custodian_of` on the dispatcher's role and as `delivered_by`/`manager`. So
`management-dispatcher` describes nothing true there, and the owner becomes `gitea-site-admin`, the issuer and
revoker, as it already is for the other standing site-administrator tokens (`cred-verifier-read`,
`cred-gitea-protection-audit`, `cred-runner-registration`). Consequence for `security/inventory.json`:
`rotation_revocation_owner` `management-dispatcher` becomes `gitea-site-admin` for the four bot tokens (the inventory
checker accepts any principal; the matrix checker requires the match). `manager` stays `management-dispatcher`
(custody is unchanged).
